# CS 250 - Software Development Lifecycle

## Course Overview

In CS 250, I explored the software development lifecycle (SDLC), with a focus on Agile methodologies and the Scrum framework. Throughout the course, I worked through different Scrum team roles while developing plans for the SNHU Travel application. This experience helped me understand how communication, collaboration, and flexibility contribute to successful software development.

## How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating user stories help with this?

I interpret user needs by first understanding what the user wants to accomplish and why that functionality matters to them. Instead of immediately deciding how to build a feature, I believe it is important to ask questions and clarify expectations. During the SNHU Travel project, customer feedback included requests for personalized travel recommendations, price limits, special deals, and different vacation types. Creating user stories helped turn those requests into smaller, more manageable development tasks.

User stories also help establish clear acceptance criteria so developers and testers can determine whether a feature meets the customer's expectations. I learned that a user story should encourage communication rather than replace it. When requirements are unclear, discussing them with the Product Owner helps prevent mistakes and unnecessary rework.

## How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

When developing programs, I prefer to start by understanding the requirements, identifying the main objectives, and breaking larger problems into smaller tasks. I believe that having a clear plan is important, but I also recognize that requirements can change throughout development. The Scrum framework showed me the value of working incrementally, receiving feedback, and adjusting the development process when necessary.

In future projects, I hope to incorporate Sprint Planning, regular communication, backlog refinement, and continuous testing. I especially appreciate the idea of reviewing progress frequently rather than waiting until the entire program is finished to identify problems. These practices can help keep development organized while allowing room for improvements and changing priorities.

## What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

Being a good team member means communicating clearly, taking responsibility for assigned work, and being willing to collaborate when problems arise. Software development often involves multiple people working on connected features, so one person's decisions can affect the entire team. I believe it is important to share progress, identify obstacles early, and ask questions rather than make assumptions.

Throughout CS 250, I learned how the Product Owner, Scrum Master, developers, and testers each contribute to the development process. Although their responsibilities differ, they must work toward the same goal. A good team member should also be open to constructive feedback, respect different perspectives, and support other team members when challenges arise. Ultimately, successful software development depends on both technical ability and effective teamwork.
